## 1. Executive context

### The company

**FACT:** FastrBuild is a founder-led services company operating India-first, with the ability to serve international clients.

**FACT:** Its earlier public positioning centred primarily on B2B relationship building, sales, brand, websites, content and associated growth services.

**FACT:** Recent hands-on work has increasingly involved AI-enabled finance, marketing, prospect research, outreach, CRM, reporting, compliance, knowledge and product or engineering workflows.

**DECISION:** FastrBuild is being repositioned around AI-native services and enterprise AI enablement while retaining relationship-led growth as an operating principle.

### The category

**DECISION:** FastrBuild is a **forward-deployed AI team**.

This category means FastrBuild works alongside leadership and functional teams, studies how work actually moves, and takes responsibility for putting useful AI-enabled workflows into production.

FastrBuild is not positioned primarily as:

- an AI strategy consultancy;
- a general automation agency;
- a software reseller;
- an AI workshop provider;
- a conventional marketing or sales agency;
- or a product company selling one fixed platform.

### Canonical positioning statement

> FastrBuild is a forward-deployed AI team for founder-led, cloud-enabled companies whose AI efforts are stuck in silos. We identify the workflows where AI can create meaningful operating leverage, deploy them inside the existing business, and either transfer ownership or operate them as a managed function.

### Canonical service promise

> FastrBuild studies how work moves through your company, then designs and implements AI-enabled workflows that create measurable operating leverage.

### Operating principle

> Human where it matters. AI everywhere it compounds.

People retain work involving judgment, relationships, accountability, ambiguity, exceptions, taste or material risk. AI carries repeatable work where speed, consistency, memory or scale compounds.

## 2. The customer problem

### Problem in the buyer's language

> “We have tried to adopt AI in different parts of our workflows, but it is happening in silos and has not produced a collective result.”

### Root cause

**DECISION:** FastrBuild treats the problem as an operating-system gap, not a tool-access gap.

The missing elements are typically:

- a workflow selected for material value;
- a responsible business owner;
- integration into existing systems;
- access to the company's operating context;
- clear allocation of work across humans, AI and deterministic systems;
- approval and exception rules;
- adoption by the people doing the work;
- production monitoring;
- and measurement against an agreed baseline.

### Buying trigger

**FACT:** The typical trigger is that leadership has declared AI a priority, individual experiments are already happening, but those experiments have not produced a dependable operating result.

### Job to be done

> When our AI efforts are fragmented and leadership cannot see collective operating value, help us identify one worthwhile workflow, put it into production safely, and establish a path to own or operate it.

### Cost of doing nothing

Potential consequences to test with each buyer include:

- continued manual connective work across systems;
- uncontrolled or inconsistent AI usage;
- tool spend without operating improvement;
- duplicated experiments across functions;
- dependence on individual employees' personal methods;
- missed capacity, revenue or service-level gains;
- and growing governance or data risk.

These are problem hypotheses until confirmed in a specific buying conversation.

## 3. Ideal customer profile

### Primary ICP

**FACT:** The intended customer is a founder-led or mid-market company that already uses cloud systems but has not operationalised AI across the business.

### Recommended operating definition

**HYPOTHESIS:** Use the following as an internal targeting guide:

- approximately 30–500 employees;
- India-first, while accepting suitable international work;
- established departments or functional owners;
- recurring operational workflows with measurable volume;
- cloud accounting, CRM, Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, project-management tools, shared knowledge systems, or comparable functional software;
- individuals using ChatGPT or similar tools and possibly isolated automations;
- no repeatable company-wide AI operating capability;
- and a founder or CEO who sees AI as an operating priority.

Employee count is a proxy, not the qualification rule. Operating readiness matters more.

### Current industry posture

**FACT:** FastrBuild is currently horizontal.

**FACT:** Relevant experience or evidence exists across technology services, healthcare or compliance, automotive software and B2B services.

**OPEN:** No permanent industry vertical has been selected.

### Buyer structure

- **Economic buyer:** Founder or CEO
- **Business owner:** Functional leader responsible for the first workflow
- **Participants as required:** Operations, technology, security and affected employees

### Minimum customer readiness

**DECISION:** Qualify based on operating readiness rather than revenue alone.

A deployable customer should have:

1. An executive sponsor with decision authority.
2. A named owner for the selected workflow.
3. A repeatable workflow involving multiple people or material recurring volume.
4. A business consequence worth measuring.
5. Accessible systems, data and subject-matter expertise.
6. Willingness to provide approvals and operating input.
7. Two to four hours of client-team participation each week during deployment.
8. Production intent rather than curiosity alone.
9. A plausible twelve-month value opportunity of at least three times the expected engagement cost.

### Poor-fit customers

- very early businesses without stable processes, departments or sufficient recurring work;
- companies with too little operating volume or economic value to justify deployment;
- organisations seeking only a generic workshop or strategy presentation;
- speculative pilots without a workflow owner or production path;
- teams unable to provide access, operating context or timely approvals;
- and buyers demanding guaranteed outcomes without a baseline or attribution model.

## 5. Offer architecture

### Offer ladder

> Diagnose → Deploy → Operate or transfer → Expand

Each engagement begins with one named workflow, one responsible owner and one measurement plan. Clients should not be asked to buy an undefined “AI transformation.”

### Offer 1 — AI Opportunity Diagnostic

**Purpose:** Identify and economically rank the first workflow worth deploying.

**Format:** Executive and workflow-owner discussion, light workflow and systems review, readiness assessment and delivery recommendation.

**Expected output:**

- named workflow;
- current-state description;
- initial baseline and value hypothesis;
- responsible owner;
- access, data, risk and approval constraints;
- and a recommendation to deploy, use a managed service, use build–operate–transfer, or not proceed.

**DECISION:** Initially free and selective.

**Boundary:** The diagnostic provides decision-quality analysis. Detailed solution architecture belongs in paid work.

### Offer 2 — AI Workflow Deployment

**Purpose:** Put one meaningful AI-enabled workflow into production inside the client's business.

**HYPOTHESIS:** Approximately six weeks for a typical first deployment. Complexity may change the duration.

**Scope:**

- observe and baseline the current workflow;
- redesign work across people, AI and existing systems;
- build or configure the workflow;
- integrate necessary systems and knowledge;
- define human approvals, exceptions, access and controls;
- run real production work through it;
- evaluate output;
- document the operating model;
- train its owners;
- and agree how performance will be measured.

**End state:** One production workflow with a clear owner, documented operating rules, human approval points, exception paths and a measurement plan.

### Offer 3 — Managed AI Function

**Purpose:** Give a client a recurring operating result without requiring it to build the full internal capability immediately.

**Suitable examples:** Managed research and outreach, content operations, reporting workflows, document or evidence operations, and other repeatable work with defined inputs, outputs, service levels and exceptions.

**Boundary:** Use this model only when the work is recurring, sufficiently standardisable, measurable and substantially within FastrBuild's control.

### Bridge — Build–Operate–Transfer

**Purpose:** Deploy the workflow, operate it through stabilisation, and transfer it once the client's owner, documentation, controls and monitoring are ready.

**DECISION:** Recommend this path when the client wants long-term ownership but needs immediate results and operating support.

### Expansion

After the first workflow, the client may:

- own and operate it internally;
- retain FastrBuild for monitoring and continuous improvement;
- keep it as a managed service;
- complete a supported transfer;
- or deploy the method into the next function.

## 7. Delivery model

Every engagement follows the same operating logic:

1. **Study** — Observe the workflow, systems, handoffs, failure modes, context and decisions.
2. **Prioritise** — Select work with enough value, feasibility, repeatability and ownership.
3. **Redesign** — Allocate work across humans, AI and deterministic systems; define approvals and exceptions.
4. **Deploy** — Build inside the existing stack and test with real work.
5. **Adopt** — Train owners, document operating rules and establish governance.
6. **Measure** — Compare production performance with the agreed baseline.
7. **Operate or transfer** — Continue as a managed service, stabilise before handover, or move to the next workflow.

### FastrBuild responsibilities

- workflow interviews and observation;
- process mapping;
- AI opportunity prioritisation;
- tool and model selection;
- custom agent and workflow development;
- integration with existing systems;
- data and knowledge preparation;
- governance and access controls;
- employee training;
- SOP and documentation creation;
- monitoring and evaluation;
- ongoing workflow operation where contracted;
- and executive AI strategy where it directly supports execution.

### Client responsibilities

- access to relevant employees, systems and infrastructure;
- accurate business rules and subject-matter input;
- a responsible workflow owner;
- review meetings and timely decisions;
- approvals, security and access support;
- and adoption by the team that will use or oversee the workflow.

FastrBuild brings AI and workflow capability. The client retains superior knowledge of its own business and remains accountable for business decisions.

## 8. Ownership models

### Client-owned forward deployment

Use when the workflow is strategically unique, sensitive, deeply embedded in internal systems, dependent on organisational change, or intended to become permanent internal capability.

The client owns its data, business systems and paid-for client-specific work inside its environment, subject to third-party rights and reusable FastrBuild components.

### Managed service

Use when:

- outputs recur and can be clearly measured;
- operating rules are sufficiently stable;
- exceptions are manageable;
- FastrBuild controls most delivery variables;
- and the client primarily wants the outcome rather than immediate internal capability.

### Build–operate–transfer

Use when the client wants ownership but needs a stabilisation period before its team can operate the workflow reliably.

### Intellectual-property principle

**DECISION:** Contracts should distinguish:

- client systems and data;
- client-specific work product;
- third-party tools and models;
- and reusable FastrBuild methods, frameworks or components.

Exact legal language remains contract-specific.

## 9. Commercial model

### Pricing principle

> A fixed commercial foundation, with value-linked pricing only where the baseline and attribution can be objectively agreed.

### Components

1. **Fixed deployment fee** — covers workflow study, redesign, engineering, integration, testing and enablement.
2. **Minimum operating retainer** — covers reserved capacity, monitoring, maintenance, evaluation and ongoing operating responsibility.
3. **Usage or volume component** — used when transaction volume materially changes delivery cost.
4. **Value-linked component** — used for verified savings, avoided hiring, increased throughput, attributable gross profit, quality or risk milestones when attribution is defensible.

### Pricing guardrails

- Never rely entirely on a success fee.
- Never link fees to outcomes FastrBuild cannot materially control.
- Prefer attributable gross profit to headline revenue when a revenue-linked fee is appropriate.
- Define the baseline, data source, measurement period, attribution rules and exclusions before work starts.
- Use caps and review points for variable fees.
- Treat hours released as operational capacity, not automatic financial savings.
- Convert hours into realised value only when they lead to avoided hiring, increased output, faster revenue, improved service levels or another observable consequence.

### Price levels

**OPEN:** Exact diagnostic, deployment and managed-service prices have not been set.

**DECISION:** Do not publish a universal fixed price until three to five real deployments establish credible complexity and delivery bands.

### Complexity drivers

- number and quality of systems involved;
- accessibility and cleanliness of data;
- workflow volume and variability;
- security and governance requirements;
- approval paths and exception volume;
- amount of custom engineering;
- and organisational change required.

## 10. Measurement and proof

### Measurement principle

> Every deployment begins with an agreed baseline and is measured against adoption, efficiency, quality and business impact.

Choose a small number of relevant measures for each engagement.

### Level 1 — Adoption

- active usage;
- percentage of eligible work using the new workflow;
- owner participation;
- and compliance with the intended operating path.

### Level 2 — Operational performance

- cycle time;
- useful hours or capacity released;
- throughput;
- cost per transaction;
- turnaround time;
- and service-level attainment.

### Level 3 — Reliability and quality

- successful run rate;
- exception rate;
- human intervention rate;
- errors;
- rework;
- consistency;
- and monitoring coverage.

### Level 4 — Business impact

- avoided cost or hiring;
- additional capacity;
- attributable gross profit;
- faster collections or revenue;
- reduced leakage;
- better control coverage;
- and reduced operating or compliance risk.

### Evidence currently available

**FACT:** Internal or sensitive evidence includes:

- Coda-based operating systems;
- n8n lead, follow-up, reporting and CRM workflows;
- AI-assisted finance operations;
- AI-enabled marketing and content production;

- sales-outreach enablement associated with GRAC.ai;
- AI-related work within Accelerate;
- Vajra governed AI infrastructure;
- and the AI Enablement Audit at audit.vajra.work.

Some names or associations may require confirmation before public use.

### Public proof boundary

**FACT:** Much client work is custom and sensitive and cannot automatically be disclosed.

**DECISION:** Do not publish client identities, specific workflow details, anonymised stories or quantified results until the disclosure format and evidence are explicitly approved.

**DECISION:** Anonymised proof stories are currently deferred.

### Claims to avoid

- “Entire functions are automated.”
- Guaranteed percentage improvements without a baseline and controlled scope.
- Enterprise-wide transformation claims based on one workflow.
- Unqualified cost-saving or time-saving claims.
- Product or capability claims that have not been deployed in real conditions.

## 11. Go-to-market direction

### Company position versus acquisition wedge

**DECISION:** Keep the company position horizontal: forward-deployed AI for founder-led, cloud-enabled companies.

**HYPOTHESIS:** Use AI-native Revenue Systems as the first 90-day acquisition wedge because it aligns with existing credibility, reaches a visible founder priority and may demonstrate value relatively quickly.

The wedge is an acquisition experiment, not the permanent definition of the company.

### Initial target for the Revenue Systems test

- founder-led B2B services, technology services or software-enabled companies;
- existing CRM, email, productivity and sales tools;
- fragmented research, content, outreach, follow-up or reporting;
- growth expectations without equivalent headcount growth;
- and enough lead, account or content volume to establish a baseline.

### Campaign promise

> Turn disconnected AI experiments across research, outreach and CRM into one production revenue workflow with a clear owner, human approvals and measurable performance.

This is a testable promise, not a guaranteed outcome.

### 90-day learning goals

- 10–15 relevant founder or functional-leader conversations;
- at least five qualified diagnostics;
- at least two paid deployment proposals;
- and at least one paid deployment, or a documented reason conversion failed.

### Decision after the test

- **Continue** when pain, qualification and viable deployment demand repeat.
- **Narrow** when demand clusters around one workflow, industry or trigger.
- **Repackage** when buyers clearly prefer managed outcomes or client ownership.
- **Change wedge** when conversations occur but production intent, authority or economic value does not.

## 13. Voice and language

### Voice hierarchy

1. Operator-led
2. Commercial
3. Founder-to-founder
4. Technical when detail creates confidence
5. Reassuring when risk or change is involved
6. Provocative in headlines, never exaggerated in claims

“Enterprise” should be communicated through rigour, governance, security, documentation and delivery discipline—not corporate jargon.

### Writing characteristics

- clear rather than futuristic;
- specific rather than expansive;
- confident without pretending certainty;
- operational rather than conceptual;
- technically credible without tool obsession;
- and anti-consulting without becoming dismissive.

### Language to favour

- production workflow;
- operating leverage;
- forward-deployed or embedded;
- named owner and agreed baseline;
- human approvals and exceptions;
- inside the existing stack;
- measured in real work;
- operate, transfer and expand;
- and one workflow, then the next.

### Language to avoid

- generic “AI transformation”;
- “revolutionise your business”;
- “automate entire departments”;
- “replace your team”;
- “AI-powered” without explaining the work;
- overly futuristic language;
- vague innovation language;
- and guaranteed claims.

### Naming convention

Use **FastrBuild** in prose. The visual wordmark may use **fastrBuild** when matching the existing identity.

## 14. Decision rules for future work

When evaluating a new service, campaign, page, proposal, partnership or product idea, ask:

1. Does it strengthen the forward-deployed AI category or pull FastrBuild back toward a generic agency?
2. Is there a named workflow and accountable business owner?
3. Can the work reach production rather than stop at a presentation or prototype?
4. Is FastrBuild able to influence the promised result?
5. Can success be baselined and measured?
6. Is the correct model client-owned, managed or build–operate–transfer?
7. Does the engagement create reusable capability or only custom effort?
8. Are human decisions, approvals and exceptions explicit?
9. Are security, access and governance proportional to risk?
10. Is the public claim supported by evidence that can actually be disclosed?

An attractive AI idea that fails these questions should not automatically become a FastrBuild offer.
