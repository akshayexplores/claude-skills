# Outgrow — What the CEO Actually Does

**For: Jiji, CEO, Acsia Technologies**
**From: Akshay Sajeev**

---

## The short version

Outgrow works or fails on one variable, and it isn't the sales team. Programs that fail have one thing in common: the CEO endorsed the idea and then delegated all of it.

This document is the list of things that can't be delegated. There are five, they take about ten minutes a day in total, and none of them is a meeting.

I'm deliberately not quoting you a growth figure. The published numbers come from distributors with two-week sales cycles, and I'd rather commit to something I can actually measure here than borrow a number that falls apart in Q2.

---

## What we're actually doing

Acsia's sales process is built to win new logos. It runs from ICP definition through to close, then hands off to Delivery and stops. There is no motion after that point.

Meanwhile Acsia engineers sit inside OEM and Tier-1 programs every week, holding relationships no salesperson could buy, next to spend nobody asks about. Those customers are buying functional safety, cybersecurity, V&V and validation work from KPIT, Tata Elxsi, LTTS, Capgemini and GlobalLogic — often while an Acsia engineer is in the building. Most of them don't know LiLA exists.

Outgrow is a small set of deliberately trivial behaviors that close that gap: asking existing customers what else they need, telling them what else we do, and following up on proposals we've already sent. Five to ten minutes per person per day.

The system tracks **swings, not hits** — how many proactive contacts happened, not how much closed. We can't control whether a customer buys. We can control whether we reach out.

## Your five obligations

### 1. Mention Outgrow twice a day
Unprompted, in ordinary conversation. In a corridor, on a call, in a review. Not a speech — a sentence. *"Did you see Priya got a referral to Continental's commercial vehicle team?"*

This is the highest-leverage item on the list. What a CEO mentions is what the organisation believes is real.

### 2. Attend the Monday huddle
Fifteen to twenty minutes, every week, visibly. Not every other week. The weeks you miss are the weeks people conclude it's optional.

### 3. Write two sentences on the weekly scorecard
Before it goes out. Name two people and something specific they did.

A scorecard from the analytics admin is a report. A scorecard with your handwriting on it is a signal.

### 4. Congratulate people publicly and by name
Micro-wins especially — a dormant account that replied, a customer who said "I didn't know you did that", a referral to another division. Peer recognition changes behavior in a way bonuses don't.

### 5. Read the success stories and push them back out
Forward one a week with a line of comment. That's the whole ask.

## What you're signing up for, honestly

**Time:** ~10 minutes a day, plus 20 minutes on Monday.

**Duration:** this needs two quarters before revenue moves. Automotive cycles are 9–18 months and run through procurement gates. If Outgrow gets judged on closed revenue in the first 90 days, it will look like a failure at exactly the point it's starting to work — and we'll kill something that was about to pay.

**What I'll ask you not to do:** don't ask for a revenue forecast before week 26. We'll have real conversion data then. Before that, any number would be borrowed from businesses with two-week sales cycles, and I'd rather tell you we don't know than give you something that falls apart in Q2.

---

## What you get to hold me to

- **Weekly:** actions logged, by person. Participation rate.
- **Week 6:** participation above 60%, six unbroken scorecards, at least one story a week.
- **Week 12:** first read on conversion rates — how many actions it takes to surface one real opportunity.
- **Week 26:** reliable conversion data, and a forecast built on Acsia's own numbers.

If participation is below 60% at week six, the book's own diagnostic says look at leadership before looking at the team. I'll tell you if that's what I'm seeing.

---

## The one thing that kills it

Managers who treat Outgrow as optional teach their teams it's optional, and that reads directly off whether you're still talking about it in week nine.

Almost everything else — imperfect lists, clumsy scripts, a CRM field that isn't ready — is recoverable. That one isn't.

---

## What I need from you to start

1. **Confirmation you're in for the five items above** — not the concept, the five items.
2. **Six to ten participants**, mostly from Delivery, and their managers told this is real work.
3. **Eight happy customers** you're comfortable with me interviewing for twenty minutes each — ideally including two outside India.
4. **A named internal successor** to run this once I hand over. I'm a consultant; a program that depends on me ends when the engagement does.
