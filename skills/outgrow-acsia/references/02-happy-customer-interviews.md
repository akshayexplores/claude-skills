# Happy Customer Interviews

The prerequisite. Nothing else in the system should start until at least eight of these are done and the team has heard them.

## What they're for

Not research. Not NPS. Not account health. The purpose is to generate **recorded evidence, in the customer's own voice, that they value Acsia and would happily buy more** — and then play it to the people who are afraid to ask.

A secondary output is testimonials you can quote in later conversations. A third is discovering what customers actually value, which is reliably not what Acsia thinks it is.

## Who to interview

Eight to twelve customers who are demonstrably happy. Do not balance the sample. This is not an attempt to measure sentiment — it's an attempt to expose the team to the top of the distribution, because the top of the distribution is what they don't believe exists.

For Acsia, prioritize:
- Long-running delivery accounts where the relationship survived a difficult program
- Accounts where a named Acsia engineer is personally trusted
- At least two outside India, so the team can't dismiss it as a local phenomenon

## Who conducts them

Not the account owner — they'll unconsciously steer, and the customer will be polite rather than candid. Best options at Acsia, in order: Jiji himself (a CEO asking carries weight and the answers are more considered), the Outgrow leader, or a marketing person with no commercial stake in the account.

## Setting it up

Keep the ask small and non-commercial. Something close to:

> "I'd love twenty minutes of your time — I'm interested in your thoughts and experiences working with us. Would that work?"

Twenty minutes is the right number. It's small enough to say yes to and long enough to get somewhere.

## The call

**Ask permission to record at the start.** The recording is the entire point — a transcript loses the tone, and tone is what changes the team's mind.

> "Is it okay with you if I record this?"

**Talk five percent. Let them talk ninety-five.** This is much harder than it sounds and is the single biggest determinant of whether the call is useful. Your job is to ask, then get out of the way.

### The questions, in order

1. **"What are some of your favourite things about working with us?"**
2. **"How does that help you?"** — the most important question on the call. The first answer gives you features. This one gives you *value*, in the customer's language, which is what you'll reuse everywhere.
3. Follow whatever they say with: *Why?* · *How?* · *What else?* · *Tell me more.* · *Why do you say that?* · *Really?* · *That's interesting.*
4. **"How many suppliers, providers or consultants do you work with?"**
5. **"If you ranked them best to worst on the things we've been talking about — [repeat their own words back] — where would you rank us?"**
6. **"Interesting. Is there anything else that puts us there?"**
7. **"Tell me how you view your relationship with Acsia. What's it like working with us?"**
8. **"Give me three descriptive words that come to mind when you think about working with us."**

### What not to ask

**Do not ask what Acsia could do better.** You will want to — it feels responsible, and every instinct in a services business points that way. Fight it. It flips the call from appreciation to critique, and a critique recording does the opposite of what you need. There are other places to gather that. This call has one job.

If they volunteer a criticism, acknowledge it briefly, note it for later, and steer back. Don't chase it.

### Closing

> "Thank you so much — this was really valuable. I appreciate it."

## What to do with the recordings

This is where the value is actually released, and it's the step most often skipped:

- **Play clips in the weekly huddle.** Thirty to ninety seconds. Especially any moment where a customer says they'd have bought more, or didn't know Acsia offered something.
- **Cut a highlight reel** for onboarding new participants.
- **Pull direct quotes** into the testimonial bank — those become the "communicating a testimonial" action in `05-communications.md`.
- **Feed the surprises into the cross-sell map.** When a customer says "we didn't know you did V&V", that's a Did You Know entry with evidence attached.

## The moment you're looking for

Somewhere in these calls, a customer will say a version of: *"I didn't know you did that."* or *"We give that to someone else."*

Capture it exactly. That single sentence, in a real customer's voice, dismantles the belief that outreach is bothering people — and it's the reason this step comes before everything else.
