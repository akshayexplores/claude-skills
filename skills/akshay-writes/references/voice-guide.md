# Voice guide

## The anchor: a practitioner talking through the work

The raw delegation reflection is the strongest long-form personal sample in this review. Akshay describes starting young, burning out, hiring experts, the relief that followed, and the unexpected cost of losing direct knowledge. The argument grows out of experience rather than announcing a polished principle first.

The texture matters: connected sentences, a conversational “See,” distinctions written in ordinary speech, an example that makes the concern visible, then a provisional conclusion. Preserve that movement. The confidence comes from encountering the problem, not from pretending to have finished learning.

## Stable tendencies versus format effects

| Tendency | Preserve | Do not infer |
|---|---|---|
| Causal explanation | Explain why a choice produces an outcome. | Every draft needs a named framework. |
| Operational detail | Say what happens, what people see and what to do. | Old procedures or policies are current advice. |
| Learning through mistakes | Let a conclusion change with the facts. | Every post needs a confession. |
| Directness | Get to the point or incident quickly. | Every essay must open with a summary. |
| Relaxed warmth | A human aside or acknowledgement. | Emoji, profanity or a quirky sign-off everywhere. |
| Distinctions | Explain a consequential difference. | Every sentence should say “not X, but Y.” |
| Cross-domain thinking | Explain an unfamiliar mechanism through a familiar one. | Every saved analogy was invented by Akshay. |

## Casual messages

The direct chat sample is brief and unpolished: clear intention, plain language, little ceremony. There is not enough evidence to prescribe habitual slang or Malayalam/English switching.

Say the thing and its reason if the reason matters. Use “I,” “you,” “we,” contractions and natural links. A request can end as a normal question. Lowercase can match a short chat, but do not insert typos as an authenticity effect. Remove unclear references and accidental repetition without converting a two-line request into an article.

## Team communication and business email

The signed check-in memo explains a shared constraint, connects consistency to growth, then sets expectations. The useful pattern is shared purpose → reason → specific expectation. The old policy, numbers and sign-off are historical content, not defaults.

Be respectful and concrete. Explain what a change affects and what happens next. Bullets suit parallel commitments; numbers suit a sequence. A client email normally needs a clear ask or next step, not a motivational conclusion. Never invent a deadline.

Workflow documentation describes access, triggers, views and notifications from the user's perspective, with an occasional reassuring aside. Preserve that ability to make a system legible. Technical details belong when they change what someone should do.

Older signed sales drafts use recipient problems, compact benefits and an invitation to talk. They overlap with team templates. Treat their structure as historical supporting evidence, not permission to repeat unsupported metrics, stale offers or generic sales phrases.

## Personal posts and reflections

When there is a real incident, a useful shape is:

- What happened or what I was trying to do.
- What I thought would work, and why.
- What the attempt revealed.
- What I am doing differently, or what remains unresolved.

This is an available shape, not a required template. A short observation can stand alone. Let uncertainty attach to the actual unanswered question; do not add “maybe” everywhere or manufacture certainty at the end.

Keep emotion proportionate to the supplied experience. Frustration or relief should arise from the event. Avoid cinematic adversity, fabricated turning points and “I learned this the hard way” without evidence.

## Edited essays and operator explanations

The website shows a more finished mode: sections, compressed distinctions, practical diagnostics and counterexamples. Many pieces begin with a specific work situation and examine its mechanism. Publishing polish is a register, not proof that all sentence-level habits belong to the unedited voice.

Use headings where they help navigation. Give enough context to follow the argument, then show what changes a decision. Include a counterpoint when it changes where advice applies, not to satisfy a ritual objections section.

Comparing the published delegation essay with its raw note shows the experience surviving while connective phrasing is tightened and the ending becomes firmer. Default closer to the original for “sound like me”; move toward the edited version for “publish-ready.”

## Spoken scripts — provisional

This review did not establish a trustworthy corpus of spontaneous speech with speaker attribution. A prepared corporate talk may be written for someone else; screenplay characters are not evidence of everyday speech.

For a spoken approximation, use conversational prose: a thought per breath, concrete examples, questions that guide attention, natural transitions. Do not simulate an accent, vocal mannerism or language-switching habit. Keep stage directions separate from spoken words.

## Humour, imagery and certainty

Humour can make a familiar operational failure visible. Reviewed scripts use absurd office consequences and a deadpan response; the raw personal note has a lighter conversational close. This supports situational humour as an option, not constant sarcasm.

Use a metaphor when it explains an actual relationship. Prefer an ordinary comparison over an ornate one. Never draw personal history from a saved third-party essay.

Be firm about established facts and tentative about an untested conclusion. Do not add self-criticism just to resemble the website. A candid boundary is more convincing than an exaggerated claim of expertise.
