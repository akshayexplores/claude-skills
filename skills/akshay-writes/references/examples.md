# Examples and contrasts

Source excerpts are verbatim. Constructed examples below are new illustrations, not historical statements by Akshay. They demonstrate expression, not facts to import into another draft.

## Raw reflection: discovery, reversal, uncertainty

Source: [Delegating was the best and worst thing I’ve done](https://app.notion.com/p/17c641c87830802cb8a6c985a15558cd).

> Things were good, we had less fuckups - it was an eureka moment. All until it came crumbling right back down at me.

> See there are two challenges with delegating

> And infact, I don’t know if this is the right thing to do yet, But until then cheers 🥂

Notice the relief, reversal, spoken transition and unresolved ending. Do not copy spelling errors or automatically add profanity. Preserve the willingness to leave a conclusion open.

Constructed brief: “I delegated reporting, saved time, then discovered I no longer understood the assumptions. I now review one report with the analyst weekly. I don't know yet if that's sufficient.”

**Too polished:**

> Delegation is a double-edged sword. True leadership requires balancing strategic oversight with operational excellence.

**Closer to the raw register — constructed:**

> I handed reporting over because it was taking up too much of my week. For a while, that worked really well. I had the time back and the reports were still coming in.
>
> Then someone asked why a number had changed, and I realised I couldn't explain it without asking the analyst.
>
> See, the work had moved off my plate, but so had my understanding of it. So now we go through one report together each week, including the assumptions behind it. I don't know yet if that's enough, but I need to stay close enough to understand what I'm making decisions on.

The draft preserves the chain of events without adding a dramatic loss or claiming the new process solved everything.

## Internal communication: purpose before rules

Source: [Risk, Reward System for Daily Check-in’s](https://app.notion.com/p/b0f7465cfbb14f79a4ab3d1cd9ec9153).

> This system has only one purpose - incentivize consistency.

The original explains the shared constraint before its rules. The style lesson is making the reason visible. The historical policy is not endorsed or reused here.

Constructed brief: “Ask the team to put blockers and who they need help from in the task before the daily review, so we can use the meeting to resolve them.”

**Generic:**

> To foster a culture of accountability and operational excellence, please proactively surface dependencies in advance of our daily alignment touchpoint.

**Closer — constructed:**

> Hi team,
>
> If something is blocked, please add it to the task before our daily review, along with who you need help from.
>
> Having that context beforehand gives us time to actually work through the blocker together.

## Explaining a workflow: event and consequence

Source: [Flow for iCreate’s Collective Dashboard](https://app.notion.com/p/f167c65c727447638a49742e7b010a93).

> Whenever a project is assigned to a particular collective all the members on this page will get notified and the project card gets added to the project dashboard for the collective.

Explain what triggers the behaviour and what the user sees. A light edit can improve readability without replacing practical explanation with abstract system language.

**Constructed explanation of a hypothetical feature:**

> Once you assign the project to a team, it appears on their dashboard and the members get notified. They can open it from there to see the brief and their tasks.

## A concise observation

Source: [Two ways to use money](https://app.notion.com/p/397641c8783081aabe62f917ef9e826c), attributed in Notion to Akshay's own tweet. The live tweet was not independently checked.

> There are two ways to use money.
>
> One is as a tool to live a better life. The other is a yardstick of status to measure yourself against others.
>
> Many people aspire for the former but spend their life chasing the latter.

A small distinction can be the whole piece. Do not expand every observation into a lesson, framework and CTA. This does not mean every draft should be aphoristic.

## A casual message should remain a message

Constructed brief: “Ask a collaborator for the rough cut today so I can review before tomorrow's call. Warm and casual. No promised review time.”

**Overwritten:**

> Hope you're having a productive day! I wanted to reach out regarding the pending creative deliverable so we can ensure seamless alignment ahead of our upcoming discussion.

**Closer — constructed:**

> hey, can you send the rough cut today? i'd like to go through it before tomorrow's call.

Lowercase follows the casual register; normal sentence case also fits. Do not make him sound like an essayist in a two-line message.

## Spoken approximation

Constructed brief: “Explain that buying an AI tool doesn't teach it the team's review standards. Don't claim a deployment result.”

> See, the tool can produce a draft. But it doesn't automatically know what your team would reject, or why.
>
> Someone still has to give it that context. Otherwise you're spending the time you saved creating the draft on explaining the same corrections again.

This is inferred from conversational writing, not a verified transcript or a claim about a particular product.

## Edited register has limits

The published [Running a Lean Team](https://www.akshay.life/darshana/running-a-lean-team) covers the delegation experience with clearer sections and a firmer synthesis. It supports an edited mode, not erasing the raw note's exploratory tone.

The public [Review Is the Wrong Place to Discover a Requirement](https://www.akshay.life/darshana/01-review-is-the-wrong-place-to-discover-a-requirement) tests its recommendation against cases where early specification adds bureaucracy. The transferable feature is a meaningful limit on the advice, not a compulsory counterargument heading.

## Sparse input must stay sparse

Brief: “Write about how my first failed business taught me resilience.” No incident or lesson supplied.

**Wrong:** Inventing a failed launch, an empty bank account or a late-night turning point.

**Useful response:**

> what happened with that business, and what did you change afterwards? a couple of rough lines is enough — i can shape it from there.

Alternatively, provide an explicitly incomplete draft if requested. Voice never fills a factual gap.
