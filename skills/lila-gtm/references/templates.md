# Asset templates

Use these as structure, not fill-in-the-blanks. Every bracket gets real content or gets cut. Every number gets checked against claims.md.

---

## 1. Cold email - first touch (US, product motion)

**Subject:** [signal-based, 4-7 words, e.g. "Six test engineer roles at {Company}"]

> [Relevance line: the signal, specific to them. e.g. "Saw {Company} is hiring six test engineers for the {program} team."]
>
> [Value bridge: the pain behind the signal, then one agent. e.g. "Usually that means test case writing is the bottleneck. Our TestFlow agent generates test cases from your requirements with ASPICE attributes attached - teams have cut test case creation time by 98%."]
>
> [Soft ask, or proof challenge. e.g. "Happy to run it on one of your specs so you can judge the output yourself. Worth 20 minutes?"]
>
> [Name, title, Acsia Technologies]

Under 120 words.

## 2. Email sequence (US) - 5 touches

| Touch | Day | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | Signal opener + one agent + soft ask (template 1) |
| 2 | 3 | Proof point - the 100-page spec result (69% less effort), one line on how |
| 3 | 7 | The proof challenge: "Send one spec, see the output in two weeks" |
| 4 | 12 | Value add - a teardown or webinar invite, no ask beyond "thought this was relevant" |
| 5 | 18 | Breakup - two lines, leave the door open |

Parallel LinkedIn connection on day 1; phone follow-up on anyone who opens touch 2 or 3 more than once.

## 3. LinkedIn sequence (Germany / EU)

1. **Connection note** (under 300 characters): "Hi {Name}, saw {Company} is moving {program} toward ASPICE Level 3. I work with engineering teams on exactly that evidence load - would be glad to connect."
2. **Value touch 1** (after accept, ~day 3): share a teardown, e.g. "ASPICE CL2 to CL3: what actually changes in your evidence base." One line on why it's relevant to them.
3. **Value touch 2** (~day 10): webinar invite or a short insight.
4. **Soft ask** (~day 17): proof challenge or 20-minute conversation.
5. **Phone** for anyone who engaged.

German-language versions for Tier-2s. No email until they've engaged on LinkedIn.

## 5. One-pager (product)

1. **Headline** - the problem in the buyer's words. "Your engineers spend most of their week on documentation the standard demands."
2. **The problem** - 3 lines. The V-model, the 60-70% non-coding effort, why it gets worse with SDV.
3. **What LiLA does** - the agents relevant to this audience, one line each.
4. **Proof** - 2-3 cleared results from claims.md.
5. **How it fits your process** - human-in-the-loop, standards attributes, on-prem option.
6. **Deployment and pricing** - SaaS or on-prem, one line each.
7. **Next step** - the proof challenge.

## 6. Agent one-pager

Same as above, but sections 3-4 cover one agent's features (from product.md) and that agent's cleared numbers only.

## 7. Webinar abstract

- **Title** names a concrete problem and promises something live.
- **Paragraph 1** - the problem, with one cleared fact.
- **Paragraph 2** - what attendees will see: the agent running on a realistic artifact.
- **Who should attend** - roles, not companies.
- **Speaker** - an Acsia engineer, with one line of credibility.

## 8. Case study (only with written customer approval)

Don't draft a named case study unless the customer has approved it in claims.md. Unnamed versions are INTERNAL only.

1. Customer type and program (what they build)
2. The problem, in their words
3. What they used LiLA for (which agent, which artifact)
4. Result - measured by the customer, with dates
5. Quote (approved)
6. What's next

## 9. Objection response (for reps)

Structure, from the `handle-objection` skill:
1. **What's underneath** - 1-2 sentences. Is it a real objection or a negotiation move?
2. **Talk track** - 3-5 sentences: acknowledge, answer using their own stated goals plus one proof point, end with a question.
3. **Proof to have ready** - each with its source from claims.md.
4. **If a competitor** - where they're strong, where this buyer's needs don't fit that strength, and one question that surfaces the gap.

Ready-made answers to common objections are in messaging.md.

## 10. Event pre-booking message

> "{Name}, will you be at {Event}? We're showing {agent} working on a real {artifact type} - {one cleared result}. Would 20 minutes on {day} work?"

Send 3-4 weeks before the event. Follow up once, a week before.

## 11. Social post (from an Acsia engineer or leader)

- Hook: one specific observation from real work. No "AI is changing everything."
- Body: what happened, what was learned, one detail only an engineer would know.
- One cleared number at most.
- End with a question to engineers, not a sales CTA.
- No hashtag walls, no emojis.
