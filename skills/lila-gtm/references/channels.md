# Channels, country rules and lead stages

## Country rules

These change what you're allowed to send, not just what works. Check before drafting.

### Germany and EU
- **No cold email into Germany.** UWG §7 effectively requires prior consent for marketing email. Do not "just run the US sequence" into German inboxes.
- Run LinkedIn-first: connection note referencing the signal, then 2 value touches (share a relevant teardown), then a soft meeting ask, then a phone follow-up for people who engaged.
- A 1:1 personal email is fine **after** a LinkedIn connection has accepted and engaged.
- German-language first touch for Tier-2s; English is fine for global Tier-1s.
- Events carry a lot of weight: Embedded World (Nuremberg, March), ELIV, Automotive Computing Conference, IAA Mobility. Pre-book 15-20 meetings per event from the signal list, no booth needed, host a side dinner for Tier-A prospects.
- Elsewhere in the EU, GDPR legitimate interest is usually read more loosely - but check the country before sending cold email.

### United States
- Full email motion is allowed (Apollo), with a parallel LinkedIn track and phone follow-up on opens and clicks.
- 5-touch email sequence: signal opener, proof point, LiLA hook, value add, breakup.
- EV-native companies respond to e-mobility and speed. Legacy Tier-1s respond to certification and cost of quality.
- Event moments: CES, SAE WCX.

### Japan
- Relationship program, not campaigns. Japanese buyers punish vendor-style cold outreach.
- Senior people do the nurturing, not SDRs. Japanese-language LiLA material.
- Event moments: Automotive World Tokyo, JSAE. Referrals through the Nissan ecosystem.
- Measure relationships opened, not MQLs.

### India and UK
- Not specifically covered in the source plans. Default to LinkedIn plus personal email, and flag the assumption.

## Email rules (all markets where email is allowed)

Adapted from the `draft-outreach` skill.

- **Under 120 words** in the body.
- **Subject line 4-7 words.** Specific, references the hook, not the product. No "LiLA" in the subject of a first touch.
- **Structure:** relevance line (proves homework, from a real signal) → value bridge (1-2 sentences connecting their situation to one agent, one cleared proof point if it fits) → one soft ask.
- **Never** "I came across your company", "hope this finds you well", "I wanted to reach out", or a paragraph of intro about Acsia.
- Multi-touch: each touch escalates in directness, adds something new (a proof point, a teardown, the proof challenge), and the last is a short breakup.
- Default ask: "Worth 20 minutes to see if this maps to what you're working on?" Stronger ask when there's real pain: the proof challenge.

## LinkedIn rules

- Connection note: under 300 characters, references the signal, no pitch.
- Value touches: share something useful (a teardown, a webinar invite), one line of context.
- Posts from Acsia people: engineer voice, specific, one idea per post. No hashtag walls, no emojis.

## Webinars

- Monthly, 45 minutes, alternating themes.
- An engineer presents to engineers, with a live LiLA demo segment.
- Target 60-120 registrants. Attendees from target accounts count as MQLs.
- Titles name a concrete problem: "Generating ASPICE-ready test cases from a real spec - live."

## Lead stages (FY27 plan definitions)

Use these words exactly. Loose definitions are how marketing and sales stop trusting each other.

- **MQL** - right role, right account type, right geography, at a Tier A/B/C account, who has taken a meaningful action: replied with interest, attended a webinar or event session, requested the proof challenge or a workshop, accepted a meeting, or engaged on a buying-relevant topic. **Not an MQL:** a LinkedIn accept, an email open, a download with no follow-up, a title match with no action.
- **SAL** - Inside Sales has had a live conversation and confirmed a real engineering need, that the contact owns it or can reach the owner, and plausible timing inside 9 months.
- **SQL** - after discovery, all four confirmed: a named **initiative** (program, RFQ, platform change, certification drive); a named engineering **owner** engaged; a decision **window** inside 6-9 months; and an agreed **next step**. Three of four stays SAL.

## Handover brief (MQL to Sales)

When asked to write a handover, include:
1. Account snapshot and the signal that triggered outreach
2. People map - who's engaged, who owns FuSa, who runs the program
3. Need statement in the prospect's own words
4. Which agent or theme fits, which references apply, whether the proof challenge was offered
5. Competitive context - incumbent vendor, in-house appetite
6. Recommended first move and timing

## Deal qualification

MEDDPICC from Stage 2 onward. Two LiLA-specific notes:
- **Decision criteria** often include supplier qualification steps - ASPICE assessment of the vendor, TISAX check, pilot first. Ask early.
- **Paper process** at German Tier-1s can take 60 days. Map it in Stage 3.

Kill signals: no named program after two conversations; champion can't reach the economic buyer; "send your rates" with no technical engagement.
