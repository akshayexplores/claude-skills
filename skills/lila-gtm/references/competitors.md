# Competitors - internal use only

**Never name a competitor in customer-facing writing** - no comparison tables, no "unlike X" on a website, no names in emails. Use these to prepare, then speak in categories ("general-purpose AI tools", "large services firms"). In a live conversation, if the prospect names a competitor, you can respond to it directly.

Competitor details below come from Acsia's internal notes and have not been independently verified. Check current facts before relying on them in a deal.

## How buyers see the alternatives

| Alternative | What the buyer thinks | LiLA's answer |
|---|---|---|
| **General AI** (Copilot, ChatGPT and similar) | "We already have AI." | Writes text and code, not certifiable work products. Usually can't take OEM specs because of IP rules. LiLA understands the standards and can run on-prem. |
| **Doing nothing / hiring more engineers** | "We'll manage." | Automotive software engineers are scarce and documentation grows with code. The paperwork grows faster than headcount can. |
| **Build in-house** | "Our AI team can do this." | The hard part is the domain knowledge of what each work product must contain, not the model. Offer the proof challenge as a fast build-vs-buy test. |
| **Large engineering services firms** (KPIT, Tata Elxsi, LTTS, Capgemini, HCL) - services motion | "We already have a vendor." | Acsia is automotive-only, and LiLA is visible proof of that depth. Senior attention at mid-size deal values. |

| Up against | Win theme | Proof to bring |

| Large pure-play (e.g. KPIT) | Senior attention and equal certifications without the bureaucracy. "Your program is our flagship; it's their rounding error." | Certification documents, named senior team in the proposal, reference call with a similar-size engagement |

| No decision | Cost of delay tied to their own program milestones. | Their hiring gaps, program timeline maths |

## Battlecard format

When asked for a battlecard, use this structure on one page:

1. **When you'll meet them** - deal signs that this competitor is in play
2. **What they'll say** - their likely pitch in two lines
3. **Where they're strong** - be honest; reps lose credibility denying real strengths
4. **Where LiLA wins** - three points, each with proof from claims.md
5. **Landmine questions** - questions to ask the prospect that expose the gap (e.g. "Can it run without any connection to an outside server?")
6. **If they say X, say Y** - the two or three most likely objections
7. **Last verified** - date and source for every competitor fact
