# Positioning, hooks, offers and objections

## Positioning statement

For engineering leaders at automotive and regulated-industry suppliers who spend most of their engineering effort on documentation, testing and compliance instead of design, LiLA is a suite of AI agents built for regulated engineering. It produces requirements, test cases, traceability and compliance artifacts with the standards built in, for engineers to review. Unlike general AI tools, it understands ASPICE, ISO 26262 and MISRA, and can run fully on-premise.

## The core story in one paragraph

A car runs on more than 100 million lines of code [cite source]. Every line that touches braking or steering goes through a lifecycle that safety standards demand, and most of that effort is documentation written by hand. As software grows, the paperwork grows with it, and hiring can't keep up. Generic AI can't help because it doesn't produce certifiable artifacts and can't touch confidential IP. Acsia built LiLA after living this problem on OEM programs. It takes the process-heavy work so engineers can do engineering.

## Three message pillars

1. **Built for the standard, not adapted to it.** ASPICE, ISO 26262 and MISRA attributes come with every artifact.
2. **Your engineers stay in charge.** LiLA drafts, they review and sign. That's how it fits a safety process.
3. **Your IP stays inside.** On-prem deployment runs fully offline on your own infrastructure.

## Objection handling

Format for every objection: acknowledge what's true, reframe, offer proof, ask a question.

**"We already use Copilot / ChatGPT."**
Fair - they're good at writing code and text. They don't produce ASPICE work products with traceability, and most teams can't legally put OEM specs into them. LiLA is built for the artifacts an assessor looks at and can run inside your network. Want to see it on one of your specs?

**"Our data can't leave our network."**
That's the norm in this industry. The on-prem licence runs fully offline on your infrastructure. Nothing leaves.

**"An assessor won't accept AI-generated artifacts."**
They shouldn't accept unreviewed ones, and LiLA doesn't ask them to. It drafts with the standard's attributes and traceability attached; your engineers review and own the output. Tool classification under ISO 26262 Part 8 stays in your process. *(Never claim LiLA is certified or pre-qualified.)*

**"We'll build this ourselves."**
Some teams try. The hard part isn't the model, it's a decade of knowing what an ASPICE work product has to contain. And your engineers are already short on time for the product. The proof challenge shows in two weeks whether it's worth building.

**"It's too expensive."**
Compare it to the test or requirements engineer hours it replaces on one program. Start with the Professional plan on one team, measure, then decide. (ROI figures are modelled - see claims.md.)

**"Is it mature enough? Who else uses it?"**
LiLA came out of Acsia's own OEM programs. Offer a reference conversation on request, subject to Acsia's approval. Don't name customers in writing until claims.md clears them.

**"Not now / no budget this year."** (the real number-one competitor)
Tie delay to their schedule: their start-of-production date won't move, and every month of manual test work is schedule risk. Ask what program milestone is next.

**"Send us your rates."** (with no technical engagement)
Often procurement fishing, or being column fodder for an incumbent's renewal. Qualify before quoting: is there a named program, and who owns it?

## Voice

- Engineer to engineer. Plain and specific.
- Concrete nouns: specs, test cases, DLT logs, RFQs, work products, assessments.
- Confident about mechanism, modest about outcomes. Show the process; let the number come from a customer.
- Short sentences. No exclamation marks.

**Banned words:** revolutionary, game-changing, cutting-edge, seamless, robust, leverage, synergy, supercharge, unleash, unlock, empower, 10x, "AI-powered" as a headline, journey, solutions (as a noun for what's sold), "enterprise-grade" (unless naming the specific control), "guarantee" (for compliance or coverage).

Exception: words inside a cleared customer quote stay as the customer said them.
